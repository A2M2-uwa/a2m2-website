Research

Clinical programmes, rigorous AI

A²M² develops and evaluates AI around clinical questions in brain and cognition, vision and eye health, cancer imaging, and vascular health and healthy ageing.

Programme 01

Brain and cognition

Multimodal models for understanding cognitive change, brain health and the clinical signals that precede decline.

Understanding change over time

Brain health cannot be reduced to a single scan or score. We combine MRI, PET and longitudinal clinical information to investigate cognitive decline and disease progression over time.

Methods with a clinical endpoint

Our work links multimodal prediction with careful neuroimaging analysis. Student research and supervision contribute to a sustained programme in Alzheimer’s disease, mild cognitive impairment and interpretable progression modelling.

Programme 02

Vision, eye health and oculomics

Computational approaches to visual fields and ocular imaging, with a growing focus on vision loss, glaucoma and eye-derived signals of health.

Visual fields as clinical evidence

Visual-field testing captures patterns of vision loss that matter to patients and clinicians. We develop computational tools to discover, quantify and interpret those patterns across eye and neurological conditions.

Growing into oculomics

Oculomics is an expanding direction for the group. We are building from visual fields and ocular imaging toward questions about how eye-derived measures can inform broader health assessment.

Programme 03

Cancer imaging and precision oncology

Imaging, segmentation and quantitative analysis for cancer research, from brain tumours to PET-based biomarkers and clinically grounded treatment studies.

From segmentation to quantitative biomarkers

Tumour contours are often the starting point for volumes, radiomic features and treatment studies. We develop and evaluate AI methods that make these measurements more efficient, reproducible and clinically interpretable.

Imaging for precision oncology

Our cancer programme spans brain tumours, glioblastoma and PET-based imaging, including work relevant to metastatic prostate cancer and PSMA PET analysis.

Programme 04

Vascular health and healthy ageing

Quantitative imaging and reproducible biomarkers for cardiovascular risk assessment and healthier ageing.

Measurements that need to hold up

Imaging biomarkers are useful only when they remain reliable across observers, methods and time. We study automated quantification and the reproducibility of the measurements that follow from it.

From vascular imaging to ageing outcomes

Our work includes coronary PET/CT radiomics and automated assessment of abdominal aortic calcification, connecting imaging-based measures with cardiovascular risk and healthy-ageing questions.

Cross-cutting capabilities

From data to clinical evidence

Every programme combines multimodal clinical data with careful evaluation and a translation pathway shaped by clinicians, researchers and students.

Multimodal clinical data We combine medical images, physiological signals and longitudinal clinical records when the clinical question demands more than one source of evidence.
Clinical language and reporting A developing programme in image-to-text, information extraction and structured clinical documentation, informed by prior student supervision.
Trustworthy evaluation Reproducibility, observer variability and clinically meaningful measures are part of the result, not a final check.
