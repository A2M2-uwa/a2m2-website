Theme 01

Medical image analysis

Finding and measuring structures in volumetric scans, where the clinically interesting object is often small, low-contrast and inconsistently outlined by human experts.

Segmentation architectures

We develop and analyse deep architectures for segmenting tumours and anatomy in multi-modal MRI, PET and CT. Recent work examines state-space models as an alternative to transformers for volumetric segmentation, asking not just whether accuracy improves but which design choices are responsible and at what computational cost.

From contours to measurements

A segmentation is rarely the end goal. It feeds radiomic features, volumes and dose calculations, so small boundary differences can move a downstream number considerably. We study how segmentation variability propagates into those measurements, using automated and manual contours on the same cohorts.

Theme 02

Vision and language

Describing visual content in natural language is something people do effortlessly and machines do unevenly. Both halves of the problem interest us: generating the description, and deciding whether it is any good.

Generating descriptions

Our captioning work has looked at the vocabulary problem — how the choice of linguistic unit changes what a model can express. Subword-informed captioning reaches competitive metric scores with a fraction of the training vocabulary of word-level models, which matters for specialised domains where terminology is long-tailed.

In medicine that long tail is the whole problem. Clinical language is dense with rare, compound and abbreviated terms, and a model that cannot represent them cannot report on them.

Judging descriptions

Captioning metrics tend to agree with human judgement when averaged over a whole system, and disagree badly on individual outputs. Our work on learned composite metrics targets exactly that gap, because per-case reliability is what a clinical setting needs.

Theme 03

Physiological signals and clinical data

Long, noisy, multi-channel recordings where the label is a clinical judgement rather than a physical measurement.

Multi-channel fusion

Sleep staging is a good test case for signal fusion: technicians score overnight studies from several simultaneous channels, and no single channel contains the answer. Our work shows that integrating information across channels improves automatic classification over single-channel baselines, and quantifies which combinations carry the gain.

Prediction from routine measurements

Not every clinical question needs an image or a waveform. We also build predictive models on routinely collected biochemical and compositional measurements, where the research contribution is as much in careful validation as in the model itself.

Theme 04

Trustworthy evaluation

The thread running through every other theme. A number reported without its variability is not a result, and a model compared against an unreliable reference cannot be ranked honestly.

Reproducibility Which derived features survive a change of observer? We assess inter- and intra-observer reproducibility directly, and report how much of a feature set is actually stable.
Observer variability Human disagreement sets a ceiling on achievable accuracy. Measuring it tells us when a model has reached the limit of the reference standard rather than the limit of the method.
Metric design For generated text and other open-ended outputs, we treat the evaluation metric as a model in its own right, trained and validated against expert judgement.
