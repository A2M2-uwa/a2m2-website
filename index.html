Our purpose

Augmentation, not replacement.

Clinical medicine generates more data than any individual can read closely: volumetric scans, overnight physiological recordings, longitudinal records, etc. Machine learning can surface what matters in that data, but a model is only useful in a clinic if its behaviour is predictable, measurable and explainable.

Our work sits at that intersection. We develop computer vision, natural language processing and signal-processing methods for medical problems, and we treat evaluation as a first-class research question rather than a final table in a paper.

Our approach

From data to clinical evidence

Across every programme, we combine multimodal clinical data, careful evaluation and a translation pathway shaped together with clinicians, researchers and students.

Multimodal clinical data We bring together medical images, physiological signals and longitudinal clinical records when a question needs more than one source of evidence.
Clinical reporting and language We develop methods that turn clinical images and data into accurate, structured language, from medical-image reporting to information extraction from clinical notes.
Trustworthy evaluation and translation We treat reproducibility, observer variability and clinically meaningful measurement as part of the result, so models can move responsibly toward clinical use.
