Our purpose

Augmentation, not replacement.

Clinical medicine generates more data than any individual can read closely: volumetric scans, overnight physiological recordings, longitudinal records. Machine learning can surface what matters in that data, but a model is only useful in a clinic if its behaviour is predictable, measurable and explainable.

Our work sits at that intersection. We develop computer vision, natural language processing and signal-processing methods for medical problems, and we treat evaluation as a first-class research question rather than a final table in a paper.

Clinically grounded

Projects are shaped with clinicians and medical physicists, on real patient cohorts and trial data.

Methods first

We publish architectures, metrics and analysis — not just applications of existing tools.

Measured honestly

Reproducibility and observer variability are part of the result, not a footnote.

Open by default

Preprints, code and trained models released wherever ethics and licensing allow.
